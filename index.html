The Turning Point

Truth never needs the fear
of punishment or death
to protect itself.

Everyone has something that compels them to explore. A geologist studies rocks, a mycologist fungi, an astronomer stars. For me, that subject is belief. I was trained as a dentist and later studied business, but I keep returning to questions about what shapes human life at a civilisational level. I call this my generative force: it drives me to question, compare, explore, and understand the belief systems that shape how we think, choose, relate, and organize society. Beliefs influence what we consider right, wrong, possible, or worth pursuing; what we fear and hope for; and what future we are willing to build together. I see humanity as the hardware and belief systems as its software. They shape individuals, communities, nations, the stories that unite us, and who we perceive as friend or enemy. The human hardware is not fundamentally broken. The problem is the software running it.

Much of that software was created thousands of years ago for a world that no longer exists. Humanity has transformed and technology has advanced, yet billions remain guided by beliefs built for another age. We have redesigned our institutions and scientific understanding, but never deliberately designed a belief system for humanity as one civilisation. Now AI is transforming society, and AGI will expand its consequences. Humanity needs beliefs that let us embrace AI rather than fear it, and guide its development toward a prosperous, abundant, and secure future for all. Without evolving our beliefs, old fears and divisions can be amplified by new technologies. The hardware needs no replacing; software must evolve. Eureka is my attempt to explore belief, truth, fear, meaning, and what it means to live as one human civilisation. It is not a religion: it has no claims, rituals, worship, or demands for faith.

01

The Awakening

It begins with the deliberate examination of what we have accepted as truth. In my personal experience, this happened through five important realisations. It was not about rejecting established beliefs, but about understanding more clearly what is true, what is believed, what is feared, what it means to find meaning, and how we choose to live together.

01

Knowledge is not Belief

We cannot know everything about life and the universe, even in ten lifetimes. Therefore, belief is unavoidable. Beliefs help us navigate what we cannot fully know, and they drive individuals and civilisations forward. But belief must never be mistaken for knowledge. Where there is knowledge, belief is no longer needed to fill the gap. Even those who claim to believe in nothing still hold assumptions about reality, truth, and what can be known. The realisation: Know what you know. Recognise what you believe. Be honest about what you do not know.

02

Reality is not our Perception of It

We encounter reality through perception, and perception is limited by our senses, minds, memories, language, experiences, exposure, traditions, and beliefs. Two people can observe the same reality from different perspectives and arrive at completely opposite conclusions. What we perceive is an interpretation of reality, not necessarily reality in its entirety. No individual perspective contains the whole of what is. The realisation: What we perceive is not necessarily all that exists. Truth does not depend upon our perception of it. To seek greater truth, we must unite opposing perspectives and see beyond what either can see alone.

03

Fear Can Protect Belief, Not the Truth

Fear does more than protect us from danger. We fear being wrong, losing our identity, facing the unknown, and ultimately facing death. These fears can shape the beliefs we hold and make us defend them rather than question them. But truth does not need fear, punishment, or the threat of death to defend itself. The realisation: No matter how many people collectively fear and believe in something, their fear does not make their belief true. No matter how powerful the fear, we must not let it prevent us from seeking what is true.

04

What Is the Ultimate Question?

The meaning of life is often understood as what we give to it. But that answers only the question of how one chooses to live as an individual. When we step beyond the individual and consider that millions of species live finite lives on one planet, around one star, among an unimaginable number of stars and worlds, without knowing whether life exists elsewhere, a greater question remains: Why are we here? The realisation: We may find meaning in our personal lives, but the meaning of existence remains an open question. And so we must continue searching for the truth.

05

We Are One Human Civilisation

Humanity is separated by nations and shaped by different cultures, religions, languages, and traditions. Some people often glorify the civilisations of the past, dividing history into East and West, one civilisation against another. But the world we live in today is increasingly one civilisation. We share technology, money, science, food systems, communication, and a common global economy. Our cultures may remain different, but our civilisation is increasingly shared. The realisation: We need not to inherit the divisions of the past. Humanity is one civilisation, and its future belongs to all of us.
