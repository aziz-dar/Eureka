WHAT IS EUREKA
The Turning Point

Truth never needs the fear
of punishment or death
to protect itself.

Most people have something that compels them to explore. A geologist studies rocks. A mycologist studies fungi. An astronomer studies stars. Nobody needs to ask why. It is simply the subject their mind keeps returning to. For me, that subject is belief. I was trained as a dentist and later studied business, but I don't find myself thinking about teeth or companies. I find myself driven to understand what shapes human life at a civilisational level, and again and again, I return to belief systems. I call this my generative force. It drives me to question, compare, explore, and understand the systems of belief that shape human life. I look at how beliefs take root, shape our choices, organise our societies, influence our relationships, and determine what we consider right, wrong, possible, or worth pursuing. They influence how we understand ourselves, how we treat one another, what we fear, what we hope for, and what kind of future we are willing to build together as a species and civilisation. I see humanity as the hardware and belief systems as its programmable software. They shape how individuals think, how communities organise, how nations act, which stories unite us, and who we perceive as friend or enemy. There is nothing fundamentally wrong with the human hardware. The problem lies in the software running it.

Much of that software was created thousands of years ago for a world that no longer exists. Humanity has transformed, our knowledge has expanded, and our technology has advanced, yet billions remain guided by belief systems built for another age. We have deliberately redesigned our technologies, institutions, economies, and scientific understanding, but we have never deliberately designed a belief system for humanity as one civilisation. The major systems we have today emerged from historical circumstances, often in response to dogmas that preceded them. Now AI is transforming society, and AGI will expand the consequences of that transformation. Our technological power is advancing faster than our collective wisdom. Humanity needs beliefs capable of supporting an abundant, prosperous, and secure future for all. We need beliefs that allow us to embrace AI rather than fear it, and that can guide the direction AI develops. Without evolving our beliefs, our oldest fears and divisions can be amplified by our newest technologies. The hardware does not need to be replaced. The software needs to evolve. Eureka is my attempt to explore belief, truth, fear, meaning, and what it means to live as one human civilisation. It is not a religion. It has no claims, no worship, no rituals, and no demand for faith.

01

The Awakening

It begins with the deliberate examination of what we have accepted as truth. In my personal experience, this happened through five important realisations. It was not about rejecting established beliefs, but about understanding more clearly what is true, what is believed, what is feared, what it means to find meaning, and how we choose to live together.

01

Knowledge Is Not Belief

We cannot know everything about life and the universe, even in ten human lifetimes, given the vastness of the universe and the complexity of life and consciousness. Therefore, belief is unavoidable. Beliefs help us navigate what we cannot know, giving individuals and civilisations a larger story to connect with and a framework for understanding the unknown. But belief is not knowledge. Belief fills the space where certainty ends, and problems arise when we mistake what we believe for what we know, turning unprovable claims into undeniable truths and even justifying wars through them. Even those who believe in nothing still hold assumptions about reality, truth, and what can be known. The unknown should not be feared simply because we cannot yet explain it. The realisation: Know what you know. Recognise what you believe. Be honest about what you do not know. Pursue the unknown with curiosity. Remain willing to question what you believe. And never let beliefs justify absurdities.

02

Reality Is Not Our Perception of It

We encounter reality through perception, but perception is limited by our senses, minds, memories, language, experiences, exposure, traditions, and beliefs. Two people can observe the same reality from different perspectives and arrive at completely opposite conclusions, while each may still be right from their own point of view. What we perceive is an interpretation of reality, not necessarily reality in its entirety. Our minds filter what we notice, what we remember, and what we consider possible, often without us realising it. What seems obvious to one person may be invisible to another, not because reality has changed, but because their perspective has. No individual perspective contains the whole of what is. The realisation: What we perceive is not necessarily all that exists. Truth does not depend upon our perception of it. To seek greater truth, we must question the limits of our own perception, unite opposing perspectives, and see beyond what either can see alone.

03

Fear Can Protect Belief, Not the Truth

Fear does more than protect us from danger. It has been a fundamental driver of human survival. Humans fear being wrong, losing identity, facing the unknown, and ultimately facing death. These anxieties reinforce inherited beliefs and make us defend them rather than question them. Our beliefs shape what we fear, and what we fear shapes how we behave. Fear has shaped humanity as deeply as reason and desire, but at the scale of civilisation, what fears drive the behaviour of the masses? If entire societies are taught to dread ideas such as the end of the world, the end times, or an approaching era of chaos, those beliefs shape collective behaviour and create some of the very conditions people anticipate. The realisation: No matter how many people collectively fear and believe in something, their fear does not make their belief true. No matter how powerful the fear, we must not let it prevent us from seeking what is true, no matter the cost.

04

What Is the Ultimate Question?

The meaning of life is often understood as what we give to it. But that answers only how one chooses to live as an individual. We often ask what the ultimate question is: Who made us? What is our purpose? What happens after death? Or is it something else? Perhaps we haven’t truly found our ultimate question yet. Because when we step beyond the individual and consider that millions of species live finite lives on one planet, around one star, among an unimaginable number of stars and worlds, without knowing whether life exists elsewhere, a greater mystery emerges. If the whole universe is the answer, what is the question? Perhaps we are searching for meaning within a reality whose origin, nature, and purpose we do not yet understand, and our questions themselves are limited by the perspective of the beings asking them. The realisation: We may find meaning in our personal lives, but the meaning of existence remains an open question. And so we must continue searching for the truth.

05

We Are One Human Civilisation

People often glorify civilisations of the past, hoping for their glorious revival, dividing history into East and West, with one civilisation against another. Humanity is separated by nations and shaped by different cultures, religions, languages, and traditions. But the world we live in today is increasingly one civilisation. We share technology, money, science, food systems, communication, and a global economy. What happens in one part of the world increasingly affects another, and the challenges we face, from climate and disease to technology and resources, cross borders. We are more interconnected than any civilisation before us. Our cultures may remain different, but our civilisation is increasingly shared. As humanity becomes more interconnected, the idea of separate civilisations will eventually give way to a single global system of governance. The realisation: We need not to inherit the divisions of the past. Humanity is one civilisation, and its future belongs to all of us.
